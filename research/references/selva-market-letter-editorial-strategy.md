# Selva Market Letter — Editorial Strategy

Use when drafting Selva's weekly newsletter / market letter for chefs, owners, F&B directors, buyers, and hospitality operators.

## Core positioning

This is not an availability email and not restaurant criticism. It is a short insider editorial about how food becomes memorable: field → grower → handling → prep → smell → sensory impact → plating → first bite.

Goal: every week Lorenzo makes chefs/operators look at one ingredient, opening, person, or sourcing habit differently.

## Audience

- Executive chefs and CDCs
- Chef-owners and owner-operators
- F&B directors and hotel/resort buyers
- Restaurant investors/operators
- Serious wholesale buyers

## Length and feel

- 3–4 minute read maximum.
- Short blocks; not a rigid template.
- Headings should be hooky and editorial, not bland labels.
- Early Popbitch-style energy: insider, sharp, forwardable, a little provocative, but never mean.
- Opinion should run through every section, not only appear in a final essay.

## Source guardrails

- Internal until Lorenzo approves external send.
- Use `selva_supplier_sourcing_map_finished.csv` / INBOUND-style sourcing map as the grower universe.
- Feature only named varieties and verified farm/grower names.
- Favor organic, regenerative, sustainable, no-spray, low-input, or minimal-intervention growers.
- Do not expose raw Natoora archive data, quantities, or competitor account names.
- Do not use anonymous commodity product in public copy.

## Recurring content pillars

The underlying structure can be:
1. Produce
2. Openings / restaurant-market signal
3. Personality to watch
4. Lorenzo's personal focus / cooking note
5. A small polarizing opinion
6. Worth tasting / quiet CTA

But do NOT use bland headings like “Produce,” “Openings,” “Personality,” or “Unpopular Opinion.” Make headings feel like hooks.

## Better heading style

Instead of `Produce`:
- `The leaf has to do something`
- `If it dies under vinaigrette, it was never alive`
- `Stop buying lettuce that collapses on contact`

Instead of `Openings`:
- `The new rooms are going tropical — the sourcing better catch up`
- `More Caribbean menus. More bad mango incoming.`
- `Miami is about to test its tropical fruit`

Instead of `Personality`:
- `Watch the connector, not the press release`
- `Some chefs move taste before they move headlines`
- `The person to watch is not always the loudest one`

Instead of `Lorenzo's Pick`:
- `Eat this raw first`
- `Don’t roast the turnip yet`
- `This is not garnish`

Instead of `Unpopular Opinion`:
- `Small heresy`
- `Nobody wants to admit this`
- `Fight me, gently`
- `Slightly dangerous opinion`

## Editorial questions to use

### Field / grower
- Who grew it?
- Was it picked for flavor or shelf life?
- Is this local because it is good, or just local because it is close?

### Handling
- Did it arrive alive?
- Does it still have snap?
- Did cold chain preserve it or flatten it?

### Preparation
- Should it be raw, cooked, grilled, shaved, preserved, juiced, zested?
- Does heat improve it or hide it?
- Does salt wake it up?
- Does acid sharpen it or overwhelm it?
- Does fat carry it?

### Sensory
- What does it smell like?
- Does it taste before seasoning?
- Is the bitterness useful?
- Does it have water, oil, crunch, perfume?
- Does it make you want a second bite?

### Plating
- Is it structure or decoration?
- Does it guide the dish?
- Does it add rhythm?
- Would the plate be worse without it?

## Polarizing opinion rules

Include one small, memorable controversial opinion weekly. It should be 3–5 lines, industry-relevant, and punch at lazy sourcing/menus, not at named restaurants or chefs.

Examples:
- `Most garnishes are apologies. If the product had flavor, texture, aroma, or purpose, it would not need to be scattered around the plate like confetti.`
- `Local is not a flavor. A bad local tomato is still a bad tomato.`
- `A serious seafood restaurant should not have a lazy citrus program. Lemon and lime are utility; Cara Cara, Meyer, Oro Blanco, yuzu, kumquat, and Makrut leaf are decisions.`

## Lorenzo's personal focus section

This is the recurring subscriber hook. It should answer:
1. What is it?
2. Why does Lorenzo care?
3. How should it be used/cooked/prepared?
4. What mistake do people make with it?
5. Who should care?

Example stance: `Hakurei turnips should be tasted raw first. Cold, thin, salt, citrus, olive oil, maybe garlic chive. If you have to roast them to make them interesting, they probably were not worth buying.`

## Voice examples

Less: `This week we are featuring local greens and citrus.`

More: `Local greens are only interesting when they do something. Tiny Farm’s baby heads and mizuna do. Most boxed lettuce doesn’t.`

Less: `Tropical fruit demand is increasing.`

More: `Miami loves tropical fruit until it has to taste like something. The restaurants that get this right will separate quickly.`
