# Lorenzo / Selva: The Walk-In naming session

## Context

Lorenzo wanted a name for a Tuesday Miami food intelligence dispatch. Earlier candidates included market/trading language such as "Market Floor" and "The Terminal Floor." He wanted something between market infrastructure and chef/kitchen culture, then pushed for more edge.

## Durable preference signal

For this class of work, Lorenzo responds to names that are:
- chef/kitchen-coded
- insider, slightly secretive, and industry-native
- edgy rather than polished food-media
- connected to market-side produce intelligence without sounding like a commodity report
- short enough to become a recurring editorial property

## Winning architecture

Tuesday dispatch:

# The Walk-In

Suggested tagline:

> Cold storage for hot Miami food intel.

Alternative cleaner tagline:

> Miami food intel before it hits the menu.

Why it worked:
- "Walk-in" is real kitchen infrastructure.
- It feels insider and a little hidden.
- It naturally holds the cold/hot contrast: cold room, hot intel.
- It can cover product, growers, chef/operator movement, openings, gossip, pricing, and recommendations.

Friday companion:

# Staff Meal

Suggested tagline:

> Where to eat, what to order, and what's worth talking about.

Purpose split:
- The Walk-In = Tuesday industry/product/chef/operator intelligence.
- Staff Meal = Friday recommendations, dishes, rooms, bars, casual insider taste.

## Section bank

Potential sections for The Walk-In:
- On The Shelf — product and growers worth watching
- Market Heat — pricing, availability, sourcing, what is moving
- On The Line — chef/operator/personality note
- 86'd — sharp contrarian take, tired trend, thing to avoid
- Where To Sit — one place/dish worth attention
- Staff Meal — closing bite or personal aside
- Back Door — off-menu/behind-the-scenes note

## Use next time

When Lorenzo asks for naming or editorial architecture around Selva/Cosecha food intelligence, start with this established system unless he explicitly wants a reset. Preserve the edgy chef-world attitude; do not drift back to generic lifestyle newsletter names.
